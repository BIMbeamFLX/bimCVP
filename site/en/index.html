Buildings last fifty years.
Software platforms don’t.

Every building creates thousands of drawings, decisions and notes. Today most of that lives inside vendor platforms with quarterly subscriptions. Gemeinwert keeps the same records signed, portable, and readable for as long as the building stands — with no platform in the middle.

Try a tool → Read why we’re building it

What it is, in plain words.

Gemeinwert isn’t a platform. It’s a thin, open way for everyone working on a building — architects, engineers, clients, contractors — to share the records they already make. Coordination topics, requirements, daily site logs, drawing approvals. The kinds of things that, in a normal project, end up scattered across five different vendor portals.

Every record is signed by the person who made it, so you can prove who said what and when, years later, without anyone’s permission. The data lives where you choose: on your own server, in your office, or with a provider you pick. Switching providers is a config change, not a rescue mission.

We don’t replace IFC, BCF, IDS or any other building standard. We use them, untouched. If Gemeinwert ever disappears, your records still open in any tool that reads the standards.

Who it’s for.

Owners and public clients

You’re paying for a building that will outlive any software subscription. We give you records that read on any tool, in any future, without trusting whoever owns the platform this year.

Architects and planners

You coordinate in Revit, Archicad, Allplan and a dozen plug-ins already. We make it possible to share BCF topics and IDS requirements directly with the people you work with — no portal between you.

Developers and integrators

MIT-licensed, plain HTML, no framework. Read the kind registry, build a tool on top, or fork the whole thing. The work is heading toward open standards drafts you can implement.
